Prototype 3 — What happens inside the intersection box?

Two-way-stop intersection (E–W major has priority, N–S minor stops), single lane per approach, HCM critical gaps (major-left 4.1 s, minor-right 6.2, minor-through 6.5, minor-left 7.1). Both panels consume identical seeded arrivals — every difference you see is the interior model.

What to look for. (1) The core trade: A decides everything once, at the stop line, and never yields again inside — fast and permissive, but push the demand sliders up and vehicles drive through each other (overlap counter: the interior has no physics). B never lets a body stop inside a conflict area it hasn't cleared — collision-free by construction, but conservative: throughput is visibly lower and queues grow at demand A handles easily. (2) Press block EAST exit: A piles overlapping ghosts; B spills back physically through the box and, at high demand, can gridlock — realism and fragility in one move. (3) In A the interior is capacity math; in B it's real estate. The engine's map contract must pick one. (4) The finding from building this: model B took four deadlock-trap fixes to survive an hour (vehicles creeping past yield walls, parking inside zones they'd committed to clear, phantom leaders on diverged paths, false merge-followers across the box). Interior-occupancy semantics are where the engineering lives — this is exactly what SUMO's internal-junction machinery is for, and what our foes/response + deterministic tie-break design must nail.
