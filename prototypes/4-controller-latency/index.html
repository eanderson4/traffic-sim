Prototype 4 — Drivers over the wire: what does controller latency do to traffic?

Same road and models as prototype 1, but every "remote" driver runs as an asynchronous controller (our NATS architecture): it observes a snapshot k ticks old, decides every m ticks, and its intent applies j ticks later. Between intents the engine holds the last commanded acceleration. The engine keeps an independent physical backstop (emergency braking, lane-change validation) — exactly the authority split in our design. In-process drivers (0 latency) are the control group.

What to look for. (1) Defaults (k=1, j=1, m=1) are the honest NATS baseline — controller sees last tick, acts next tick. Compare against remote share 0: is there any visible difference? (There shouldn't be much — 0.25 s is inside human reaction time.) (2) Push k+j to ~10 (a 1 s round trip): waves grow from nothing, backstop engagements climb — the architecture's stability budget, measured. (3) Decision cadence m is the cheap knob: m=5 with k=j=1 approximates SUMO's actionStepLength — how much does it buy before it hurts? (4) The engine/controller authority split is doing real work here: engine-side validation is what turns stale lane-change intents from collisions into rejections (counter above).
