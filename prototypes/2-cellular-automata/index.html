Prototype 2 — Cellular automata: Nagel-Schreckenberg

Same road (1200 m, 3 lanes + on-ramp) but the world is a grid: 7.5 m cells, speeds 0–5 cells/step, timestep locked to 1 s by the model itself — model and discretization are welded together. Rules: accelerate +1, clamp to gap, dawdle with probability p, move.

bottom: x–t heatmap, same convention as prototype 1 (dark = congested). NaSch famously produces correct wave speeds (≈ −1 cell/step ≈ −27 km/h at these parameters) despite the crude vehicle motion.

What to look for. (1) Motion is honest here — 27 km/h speed quanta, 7.5 m position jumps, 1 s ticks. Imagine steering this as a human player. That's the core argument against CA for our vision. (2) But: phantom jams and correct wave physics emerge from three lines of rules, and the benchmark button shows the raw speed ceiling — this is why TRANSIMS simulated entire cities with CA in the 90s. (3) The heatmap is qualitatively the same as prototype 1's — macroscopic observables survive the brutal discretization (that's the LWR bridge insight). (4) Fork essence: in CA, tick length, cell size, and model are one inseparable choice. Our continuous engine keeps them independent.
