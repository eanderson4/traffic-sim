## Blocker

**B1. Distance conservation breaks when a vehicle is observed at `S > Lane.Length` — the routine case on netimport/OSM nets.** `metrics.go:884-887` (splitTick prev-share clamp) and `metrics.go:1052-1055` (exitShares clamp).

The kernel assumes `prev.s ≤ prev.lane.Length` at every observation. That invariant does not hold: `boundaries()` (engine.go:388-412) is a single descending-index pass, and a crossing sets `v.S -= lane.Length` with no re-clamp and no cascade unless the successor has a *lower* index. netimport sorts **all internal edges after all normal edges** (netimport.go:143-156), so junction-internal lanes get the *highest* indices — a vehicle crossing feeder→internal is never reprocessed in that pass and is observed parked on a 0.1–4 m internal lane with `S` up to ~3.3 m past its length. That is the common case on exactly the I-280-class nets the addendum cites, while the multi-hop cascade the kernel handles carefully is the rare (index-inverted) case.

The double-booking mechanics: at tick T, `splitTick(A, J)` books the arrival share `v.S` (say 3.2 m) to the 0.1 m internal lane J — conserved at T. At T+1, crossing J→B: prev share `J.Length − prev.s = −3.1` is clamped to 0, and the arrival share on B (`v.S`, which embeds the 3.1 m overshoot) books in full — the overshoot is booked twice. Same on the despawn path: `exitShares` clamps the negative remainder to 0 and then books every chain lane's *full* length. VMT, per-lane interval sums, and trip distances all inflate together (so the in-repo conservation tests still pass — they check internal consistency, not ground truth), silently, with no `DroppedCrossings`. Without the clamp the −3.1 share is exactly the refund that makes J net to its true length; the clamp was defensive and is what breaks conservation. `TestKernelMultiBoundaryConservation` only covers the cascade case (`prev.s ≤ Length`), never the parked-overshoot case. Fix direction: carry/refund the overshoot (allow the negative prev share, or subtract `max(0, prev.s − prev.Length)` from the arrival share), and add a parked-overshoot test with netimport-style ordering (feeder low index, tiny internal high index).

## Should-fix

**S1. Depth-cap pruning in `findChains` is silent.** `metrics.go:989-996` — `truncated` is set only when the chain-*count* cap (`len(chains) == capN`) is hit; `len(path) > maxCrossDepth` returns without a trace. A real chain deeper than 8 hops coexisting with one shallow match books the shallow chain with `ambiguous = false` — silent misattribution, contradicting both the doc comment on `crossedChain` ("Hitting the enumeration cap also sets ambiguous") and the loudness pin. One-boolean fix: record depth-cap hits into `truncated`.

## Nit

**N1. `spawnerBacklog`'s lag term is dead code.** `metrics.go:1095-1105` — the pairing guard forces tick-by-tick observation, so an origin can only *become* overdue at `st.tick == tick` (lag 0); `max(1, lag·dt·rate)` always yields 1 in any legal run. Harmless, but the addendum presents `max(1, lag·dt·rate)` as live semantics.

**N2. `AddInitialVehicle` after `NewKernel` (both legal at tick 0) books placement position as traveled distance.** `metrics.go:292` guards `Tick != 0` but not post-construction seeding; such a vehicle lands in the first-observation branch (`metrics.go:403`) with `dist = v.S` — the phantom-placement bug the seeding exists to prevent. Worth one doc line ("kernel construction is the last pre-run mutation") or a vehicle-count check in the first Observe.

## Question

**Q1.** Given B1, is the right fix altitude the kernel, or should `boundaries()` cascade crossings regardless of index order (making `S ≤ Length` a real engine invariant)? The latter would also simplify the kernel's multi-boundary machinery — but it changes CRC streams, so it's an ADR-0005-adjacent decision, not a metrics patch.
