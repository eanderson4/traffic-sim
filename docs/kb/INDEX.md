- [x] [ADR-0031](decisions/ADR-0031-permissive-green.md) — ACCEPTED: permissive green (`g`) yields; protected green (`G`) does not. `mapSigChar` folded both into `SigGreen` — right for "may I pass the line", wrong for "who gives way" — and because signalised internal lanes carry `RowNone`, `rowGate` returned before `rowConflict` on ANY green, so the yield that defines a permissive movement never ran. Every permissive movement in every signalised network behaved as protected. On chi-loop-urban that was 2,008 of 13,181 signal links, all with foes (1,460 crossing + 5,384 merge relations evaluated by nothing); junction 256591534 alone booked 66% of a 90-minute run's overlap observations. **Correction (same day): the ADR's claim that this is why the network never cleared is FALSE** — on the fixed engine the drain has the identical shape and overlaps rose 790,454 to 2,518,364; correct semantics, wrong causal story. Kernel-only: no schema or contract change, the `g`/`G` was already in the compiled files. **Invalidates every Chicago measurement made before it** — re-measure, do not adjust
- [x] [ADR-0032](decisions/ADR-0032-routed-branch-lookahead.md) — ACCEPTED: car-following looks down the branch the vehicle is ROUTED to. `leaderAt` walked `cur.Successors[0]` past an empty lane, so a vehicle approaching a diverge watched the queue on a road it was not taking: measured on chi-loop-urban, one vehicle read a leader 831 m away at 17 m/s down the default branch while its own branch held a standing queue 19 m past the junction, its lookahead collapsing 831 m to 19.4 m in the single tick it crossed. Stopping from 20 m/s needs 22.2 m; it ended 2.83 m inside the queue tail and sat there 44 s — 437 of that run's 801 collision observations (55%) from ONE pair of cars. The same error was already fixed for measurement (`updateStats`) and junction entry (`exitBlocked`); car-following was the last place reading the default branch. Validated: 801 to 48 observations over 12,000 ticks. Kernel-only; single-successor networks bit-identical
- [x] [ADR-0033](decisions/ADR-0033-permissive-mutual-yield.md) — ACCEPTED: the foe's SIGNAL is part of its priority class. ADR-0031 made permissive approaches minor so they would yield, but `foeApproachBlocks` decides whether the FOE yields from `foe.Row`, and a signalised internal lane carries `RowNone` — read there as "unmodeled, has priority". Two permissive greens that are each other's foes therefore each yielded to the other FOREVER, with the vehicle-ID tie-break that exists to resolve exactly this sitting unreachable behind the `foeYields` test. Reproduced in isolation: one junction, one green phase, no amber or red, 120 s, neither vehicle ever moves. This is the non-clearing Chicago network — every one of the ten worst delay lanes feeds a signalised cluster junction. Fix: red foe cannot commit (skip), permissive foe is yield-class (ID tie-break decides), protected/amber/unsignalled keep priority. Unsignalised junctions bit-identical. **Its "Not done here" diagnosis of the residual gridlock was wrong and is corrected in place — see ADR-0034**
- [x] [ADR-0034](decisions/ADR-0034-gridlock-escape.md) — ACCEPTED: gridlock is a MODELLED FAILURE MODE with a bounded escape. Gridlock is a circular wait on lane capacity — A waits for room ahead, whose head waits for room ahead of IT, around a closed cycle — and every gate in the chain is answering correctly, so nothing that reasons one junction at a time can see it or prevent it. Measured: of 427 lanes frozen with vehicles aboard and zero distance travelled in the base33 final interval, **403 are ordinary ROADS against 24 junction internals**, which refutes ADR-0033's unreserved-exit-room hypothesis (that straddle is real but 6%). Reproduced in a four-junction ring where no box is ever blocked. Decision: a vehicle stopped >`StrandAfterS` (default 300 s, SUMO's) at the head of its lane AND `boxBlocked` at the junction it is routed into is removed and counted as STRANDED — network-wide, by section, and as an incomplete flagged trip record. The `boxBlocked` condition is the whole discriminator and was found by measurement: head-of-lane alone stranded 8 vehicles on the I-80 M3 freeway fixture, where the queue was ordinary. Inert on healthy networks (all M1–M3 CRCs bit-identical); a red light is never a trigger
- [ ] [ADR-0035](decisions/ADR-0035-batched-intent-log.md) — PROPOSED: the record plane wrote ONE JetStream message per applied intent, i.e. one per vehicle per tick at 10 Hz, and ADR-0026's TSIB batching stopped at that boundary by explicit scope note — the driver sends one ~150 KB batch per tick and the recorder re-explodes it into ~3,500 messages, re-paying exactly the framing the batch eliminated. Measured on the shipped chihalf recording: an 8 MiB stream block holds 34,794 intent messages over 10 ticks, ~230 B per message for a 61 B fixed record, and the whole 90-minute run is 48 GiB (~120 GiB across `data/recordings/`). Decision: TSLB v1 on a NEW subject `ts.{run}.log.intents` carrying one tick's records byte-identical to the per-message payloads they replace (one shared record codec, so the migration is checkable by construction), split by byte budget with no chunk index because stream order already IS application order; plus S2 storage compression on the stream, measured 6.2x gzip-3 / 7.2x zstd-3 on a real block. Per-record density (hoisting the repeated applied_tick and controller name, as TSIB does) is REJECTED on measurement: those bytes are what the compressor eats. Both default on, both revertible by flag (`-log-batch`, `-store-compress`). **Does not fix the harder wall, which is RAM**: a 54,000-tick run was OOM-killed at tick 38,200 on a 123 GB machine because the engine's in-memory intent log retains ~225M structs, and `-intent-log=false` is a requirement at that horizon rather than a tuning knob

- [x] [ADR-0036](decisions/ADR-0036-congestion-adaptive-routing.md) — ACCEPTED, **default ON since 2026-07-31** (addendum): congestion-adaptive routing. Static all-or-nothing routing gives vehicles no way to spread around a jam; measured on chi-loop-urban seed 42 the network dead-stops by minute 65 and never recovers. Per-lane travel-time EMA from dwell samples (capped at StrandAfterS, floored at free flow, strands included), frozen per 60 s epoch into keyframed `ttSnap` weights; next-hop tables recompute per epoch, unmetered and always current-epoch, hysteresis against the STATIC free-flow table (realized mixed-chain cost, margin max(30 s, 15 %)), constructive acyclicity. Every table is a pure function of (frozen weights, network) — bit-exact across mid-run restore, no hysteresis history to keyframe. Keyframe v6 (flag-gated; flag-off byte-identical); adaptive state folds into the CRC. Six review rounds: hysteresis-history replay violation, serve-stale metering, cycle-via-stale-static-hop, first-use bypass, and the RunMeta/TSKF contract notes all caught and fixed pre-commit. **Validation (2026-07-31): two-regime.** On survivable demand (seeds 1000–1003) adaptive wins everywhere — +22.7 % mean speed, +24 % completions, p=0.0006, d=7.77. **Measured limit: on the seed-42 6 h drain at ~2x sustained oversaturation adaptive does NOT prevent the dead-stop (786 vs 766 completions, dead from ~min 90 both ways) — there is nowhere to divert TO; rerouting spreads congestion, it does not create capacity.** Scenario manifests opt back to the static baseline (the docs/show arms were measured on it) with `adaptive_routing: false`.
